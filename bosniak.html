Bosniak classification v2019

An imaging classification, not a numerical calculator. Learn the morphology, the evidence for IIF surveillance, and the pitfalls in quoting malignancy rates.

What Silverman et al. changed in 2019

The Radiology 2019 proposal formally incorporated MRI, specified measurable imaging features and clarified categories. Thin means ≤2 mm, minimally thickened 3 mm, thick ≥4 mm, and “many” septa means ≥4. IIF includes smooth minimally thickened enhancing walls/septa or many thin enhancing septa; III includes thick or irregular enhancing walls/septa; IV includes enhancing nodules. MRI has additional qualifying appearances.

Why follow Bosniak IIF?

The F stands for follow-up. Surveillance aims to detect a change in morphological class, not merely an increase in size. An enhancing nodule, irregularity or increased wall/septal thickness may warrant reassessment. Importantly, progression to III/IV does not prove a previously benign cyst became malignant: a cancer may have been present but not initially recognisable.

Radiological upgrading: a 2023 AJR study of 246 Bosniak v2019 IIF lesions found 4% upgraded during follow-up. Selected surgical malignancy rates: Silverman et al. described the profound selection bias in resected IIF lesions; a malignancy proportion in operated cases is not the risk of transformation in all surveilled IIF lesions. The true prevalence is uncertain.

Suggested follow-up: the 2023 Canadian Urological Association guideline suggests imaging at 6–12 months in the first year, then annually if stable, for a total of five years. This is conditional guidance, not a universal proof that all lesions need exactly five years.

Examiner trap: distinguish (1) prevalence of malignancy, (2) radiological class progression, (3) clinically aggressive disease and (4) true malignant transformation.

Short viva

Question: “A 62-year-old has a 3 cm Bosniak IIF lesion. Why not remove it, and what would make you change management?”

Model answer: “IIF is probably benign but has sufficient imaging complexity to merit surveillance. I would confirm a technically adequate v2019 CT/MRI classification, discuss uncertainty and competing risks, and follow morphology according to a local evidence-based protocol. Upgrade to a higher category, especially an enhancing nodule, would trigger MDT reassessment. I would not quote a malignancy percentage from surgically selected cysts as the chance of malignant transformation.”
