Bosniak classification v2019

Imaging-based classification, not an arithmetic score. Based on Silverman et al., Radiology 2019.

For clinicians' education only. Illustrations are original schematics, not diagnostic CT/MRI. Classification requires appropriate contrast-enhanced renal imaging and radiology interpretation.

Visual comparison

Illustrative morphology only: do not infer an individual class from the drawing. Categories depend on measurable enhancing features and specific CT/MRI criteria.

Examinable v2019 criteria

ClassTypical defining featuresRevision interpretation
ISimple cyst; thin smooth wall ≤2 mm, no septa or nodule.Benign; no routine follow-up.
IIFew (1–3) thin ≤2 mm septa, which may enhance; additional benign CT/MRI-defined entities.Benign; no routine follow-up.
IIFMany (≥4) smooth thin ≤2 mm enhancing septa, OR smooth minimally thickened 3 mm enhancing wall/septa; specific MRI T1-hyperintense pattern also qualifies.Follow imaging for morphological upgrading.
IIIEnhancing thick ≥4 mm or irregular wall/septa, without qualifying enhancing nodule.Indeterminate; management depends on size and clinical context.
IVEnhancing nodule: acute-margin protrusion of any size, or obtuse-margin protrusion ≥4 mm.High malignancy likelihood; management individualised.

General applicability: cystic renal masses contain <~25% enhancing tissue; exclude a predominantly solid necrotic tumour. v2019 explicitly includes MRI. Source: Silverman et al. 2019.

Why follow IIF?

F means follow-up, not malignant transformation. The purpose is to detect evolution into more suspicious enhancing architecture (thick/irregular septa or a nodule), while avoiding unnecessary surgery for lesions that remain stable.

v2019 IIF lesions upgraded in one surveillance cohort

4%

9/246 lesions; median follow-up 28.4 months (AJR 2023).

CUA 2023 estimate of IIF progression

~5%

Recent series, versus older estimates ~15%.

Suggested follow-up: CUA 2023: image at 6–12 months during the first year, then annually if stable, typically for five years. This is a low-certainty/consensus approach, not a proven optimal schedule. One 2023 cohort observed no upgrades at the initial six-month assessment, questioning that particular time point.

Risk of malignancy ≠ risk of progression ≠ malignant transformation

Some IIF masses may already harbour indolent malignancy at baseline. Histological malignancy proportions are inflated by selection for surgery, whereas imaging-followed cohorts include many stable unbiopsied lesions. Neither statistic is the probability that a benign cyst becomes malignant.

2025 systematic review/meta-analysis: Bosniak IIF malignancy proportions were 41% in cohorts using histopathology as the reference standard, versus 2% in cohorts using imaging follow-up. These are method-dependent estimates, not interchangeable patient-level risks.

2023 CUA review: approximately 28% of resected IIF lesions were malignant, while a sensitivity calculation including conservatively managed lesions suggested approximately 7%; both estimates are subject to verification bias.

Examiner trap: “2F has a 28–41% risk of malignant transformation” is an incorrect interpretation of surgical pathology series.

Silverman et al. — the paper to quote

Silverman SG et al. Bosniak Classification of Cystic Renal Masses, Version 2019: An Update Proposal and Needs Assessment. Radiology. 2019;292:475–488. DOI 10.1148/radiol.2019182646.

The revision formally incorporated MRI, standardised wall/septal/nodule definitions and sought to improve specificity, reproducibility and reduce unnecessary surveillance/intervention.

Short FRCS viva

“A 65-year-old has a 3 cm cystic renal lesion with four smooth 2 mm enhancing septa, no nodule. What Bosniak category is this, why would you follow it, and what risk percentage would you quote?”

Model response: Bosniak IIF v2019, assuming otherwise appropriate imaging features. Explain surveillance for morphological progression, not presumed malignant transformation. Quote low upgrade rates (~4–5% in recent cohorts), distinguish this from malignancy prevalence in surgically selected cases, and discuss a five-year imaging pathway with local radiology guidance.
