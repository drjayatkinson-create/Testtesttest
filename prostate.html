3. UCSF-CAPRA (0–10) — pretreatment prognostic score

Separate from CPG. CAPRA combines PSA, Gleason score, age, clinical stage and percentage of positive biopsy cores. It is a prognostic score, not a biopsy-indication tool.

Cooperberg MR et al. The UCSF Cancer of the Prostate Risk Assessment (CAPRA) score: a straightforward and reliable preoperative predictor of disease recurrence after radical prostatectomy. Primary publication search. Score bands: 0–2 low, 3–5 intermediate, 6–10 high. The age cut-off is ≥50 years. Original CAPRA validation categories begin at PSA 2 ng/mL; values below that range are not scored by this educational implementation.

4. Prostate cancer TNM staging

Clinical T stage, MRI stage and pathological T stage are distinct. The schematic below illustrates the T/N/M categories; use the definitions and radiological staging reference beneath it.

Primary tumour (T)

T1: clinically inapparent, not palpable (T1a ≤5% of resected tissue, T1b >5%, T1c needle biopsy).
T2: organ-confined (T2a ≤half one lobe; T2b >half one lobe; T2c both lobes, in traditional clinical subdivisions).
T3a: extraprostatic extension, including microscopic bladder neck invasion.
T3b: seminal vesicle invasion.
T4: fixed or invading adjacent structures other than seminal vesicles.

Regional lymph nodes (N)

N0: no regional nodal metastasis.
N1: regional pelvic lymph node metastasis.

Distant metastases (M)

M0: no distant metastasis.
M1a: non-regional nodes.
M1b: bone.
M1c: other distant sites, with or without bone disease.

Radiological staging reference: Radiopaedia: prostate cancer staging. Schematic supplied by the user; schematic is educational and not a substitute for the staging definitions.
