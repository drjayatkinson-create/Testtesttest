NICE NG131: Do not automatically offer biopsy on PSA alone; consider DRE, comorbidity, family history, previous biopsy and MRI. NG131 §1.2. See NICE NG12 §1.6.3 for referral context.

NICE NG12 §1.6.3. Check UTI/prostatitis, recent instrumentation and other transient causes of PSA elevation; review DRE and symptoms. An abnormal DRE or strong clinical suspicion is not negated by a low PSA. Use NICE NG12 referral guidance rather than a local PSA-repeat or urgency rule.

2. MRI + PSA density: risk-adapted biopsy matrix

PSA density = PSA ÷ MRI-measured prostate volume. Enter PI-RADS below; the PSA and MRI volume are automatically taken from Section 1. NICE uses Likert 1–5; a PI-RADS category should not be silently relabelled as a NICE Likert score.

Guideline distinction: NICE offers biopsy for MRI Likert ≥3 and considers omission for Likert 1–2 after shared decision-making. EAU uses PI-RADS and PSA-density risk stratification. The table below reproduces the EAU 2026 risk-adapted decision matrix alongside the detection proportions.

3. Cambridge Prognostic Group (CPG) — NICE

Applies after prostate cancer is diagnosed. Uses PSA, Gleason grade group and integrated T stage; it is not a pre-biopsy risk model.

NICE NG131 Table 1, recommendation 1.2.15. NICE Table 1 uses PSA 10–20 ng/mL inclusively for CPG 2/3, and PSA >20 ng/mL for a high-risk feature. The calculator applies these exact boundaries. Primary guidance.

4. UCSF-CAPRA (0–10) — pretreatment prognostic score

Separate from CPG. CAPRA combines PSA, Gleason score, age, clinical stage and percentage of positive biopsy cores. It is a prognostic score, not a biopsy-indication tool.

Cooperberg MR et al. The UCSF Cancer of the Prostate Risk Assessment (CAPRA) score: a straightforward and reliable preoperative predictor of disease recurrence after radical prostatectomy. Primary publication search. Score bands: 0–2 low, 3–5 intermediate, 6–10 high. The age cut-off is ≥50 years. Original CAPRA validation categories begin at PSA 2 ng/mL; values below that range are not scored by this educational implementation.

T2a ≤half one lobe; T2b >half one lobe, not both; T2c both lobes. These are clinical/DRE subcategories in the source deck.

N0 no regional nodes; N1 regional pelvic lymph-node metastasis.

M1a non-regional nodes; M1b bone; M1c other distant sites.

FRCS trap: clinical T stage, MRI suspicion and pathological stage are not interchangeable. NICE CPG uses an integrated T stage; the traditional cT classification in the supplied slide is based on DRE. Source: EAU TNM material in your staging deck.
